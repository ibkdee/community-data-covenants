    Comparative research

    Privacy and access are competing outcomes

    The paired synthetic experiment compares persistent cached permissions, three fixed expiry durations and a sensitivity-dependent policy. Short expiry windows can reduce post-withdrawal access while denying legitimate requests during prolonged outages. The experiment isolates this trade-off; it is not a field estimate or a validation of the reference model.

    Revocation transport

    Send changes in permission before bulk uploads

    Under constrained connectivity, a small withdrawal message can wait behind a large research upload. The transport study compares FIFO and revocation-first scheduling using identical contact times and byte budgets. A scoped local gateway checks message integrity and prevents synthetic reads after a received withdrawal.

    The evaluation tracks both withdrawal delivery and ordinary upload completion, including unfinished transfers. Prioritisation may benefit consent enforcement at the expense of upload timeliness.

    Measured transmission trade-off

    Across 1,000 paired synthetic episodes, prioritising withdrawal messages reduced mean post-withdrawal reads from 421.10 to 73.93 (82.4%), while mean completion time among completed ordinary uploads increased by 28.14 minutes. Both schedules completed 884 ordinary uploads.
